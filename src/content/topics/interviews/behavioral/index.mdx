## Why it matters

Behavioural and leadership questions were the most common theme in the interview reports analysed for this site: about **23%** of 4,430 company-tagged reports mention them, across 57 of 68 companies. Many candidates prepare only algorithms and lose offers here — or get levelled lower — because their stories don't show judgement or impact. Behavioural rounds are also the most preparable: your stories already exist, they just need shaping.

<Callout type="analogy">
A behavioural interview is a reference check you give yourself. The interviewer can't watch you work for a month, so they ask for recorded footage: specific past situations, what you did, what happened. Highlights reels ("I'm a great team player") don't count; footage does.
</Callout>

## How it's scored

Interviewers map your answers to a rubric of competencies (or company values) and note the **level of scope** your stories show: a task, a project, a team, several teams. Typical signals:

| Competency | Strong evidence | Weak evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Noticed a problem nobody owned and drove it to done | "It wasn't my area, so I raised it" |
| Judgement | Weighed options with data, explained the trade-off | "We just went with X" |
| Collaboration / conflict | Disagreed with data, listened, reached a decision, kept the relationship | Avoided conflict, or "won" by escalation |
| Handling failure | Owned the mistake, fixed it, changed the process | Blamed others, or chose a fake failure |
| Impact | Measured outcome for users or the business | Activity without results |
| Growth | Clear reflection, applied later | No learning |

Values-based loops make the rubric explicit — Amazon publishes its 16 [Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) and assesses every round against a subset of them. Read the company's published values and tag your stories against them.

## The STAR answer

<Walkthrough id="star-story" />

- **Length**: about 2–3 minutes; Situation and Task under 30 seconds.
- **"I", not "we"** — the interviewer is hiring you; say what *you* did, while crediting others.
- **Specifics** — names of systems, numbers, dates. Specific answers are believable answers.
- **End with reflection** — what you learned or would do differently.

## Build a story bank

<Steps>

1. **List 10–15 candidate memories**: incidents, launches, disagreements, mistakes, mentoring, ambiguous projects, times you pushed back, times you learned something fast.
2. **Pick 6–8 with real stakes and numbers**, recent (last 2–3 years), where you personally acted.
3. **Write each as STAR** with the numbers, a one-line hook and the two follow-ups you'd least like to get.
4. **Map stories to themes** so you can answer any question with a story you know well.
5. **Say them out loud** — record yourself or do mock interviews ([Mock practice](../mock-practice/)); cut until each fits in three minutes.

</Steps>

## Common questions, grouped by theme

| Theme | Typical phrasings |
|---|---|
| Conflict | Disagreed with your manager / a senior engineer / another team; a difficult colleague |
| Failure | Biggest mistake; a project that failed; a time you missed a deadline |
| Ownership | Went beyond your role; fixed something nobody owned; on-call incident |
| Ambiguity | Requirements were unclear; had to decide with incomplete data |
| Influence | Convinced others without authority; drove adoption of a tool or practice |
| Customer | Changed a plan because of user feedback; balanced user needs with deadlines |
| Delivery | Tight deadline; cut scope; unblocked a stuck project |
| Growth | Learned something quickly; feedback you received and acted on; mentoring |
| Motivation | Why this company / team; why leaving; what you want next |

## Handling follow-ups and tricky moments

- **"What would you do differently?"** — always have an answer; "nothing" signals no reflection.
- **"What did your manager think?"** — be honest about disagreement and how it resolved.
- **No perfect story?** — say so briefly and use the closest one; don't invent. Interviewers probe details and invented stories fall apart.
- **Failure stories** must be real failures with real consequences, followed by what you changed.
- **Ask for a moment** to pick the right story — a five-second pause beats a rambling start.

<Pitfalls>

- **Hypothetical answers** ("I would…") to "tell me about a time" questions.
- **"We" all the way through** — the interviewer can't tell what you did.
- **Ten minutes of background** before any action.
- **No numbers or outcome.**
- **Fake weaknesses and failures** ("I work too hard").
- **Badmouthing** former colleagues or employers.
- **Reusing one story for everything** — prepare breadth.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Behavioural rounds score evidence of past behaviour against a rubric of competencies.
- STAR, with most time on your specific actions and a measured result plus reflection.
- A story bank of 6–8 real stories covers almost any question.
- Rehearse out loud and prepare for follow-ups; honesty beats polish.

</KeyTakeaways>
