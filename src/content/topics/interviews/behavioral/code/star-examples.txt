QUESTION: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a senior engineer."

SITUATION (≈15%)
"Our order service needed to notify five downstream teams. The tech lead proposed each team
poll our database every minute."

TASK
"I owned the integration and had to get something shipped within the quarter."

ACTION (≈60% — say "I", be specific)
"I was worried about load and coupling: five teams reading our tables would freeze our schema.
Instead of arguing in the meeting, I measured — polling would add ~300 queries/s at peak
on our primary. I wrote a one-page comparison: polling vs an outbox + Kafka topic, with cost,
effort (two weeks vs one) and failure modes, and asked for 20 minutes to review it with him.
He raised a fair point — the teams didn't run Kafka consumers yet — so we agreed on Kafka
plus a small adapter that two teams could poll during migration."

RESULT (numbers)
"We shipped in five weeks. Primary DB load stayed flat, and two more teams subscribed later
without any work on our side."

REFLECTION
"I learned to bring data and a written proposal rather than debate live, and to look for the
part of the other person's concern that's right."

QUESTION: "Tell me about a mistake you made."

SITUATION
"I wrote a migration that backfilled a new column on a 200-million-row table."

TASK / ACTION — the mistake
"I tested it on staging with 1% of the data and ran it in one transaction in production.
It locked the table, and checkout errors spiked for eight minutes before I cancelled it."

ACTION — the recovery
"I cancelled the migration, confirmed the rollback, and posted in the incident channel
with what happened. Then I rewrote it as batched updates of 10,000 rows with a pause,
ran it off-peak, and added a migration checklist item: estimate lock time on production-sized data."

RESULT
"The batched version finished in 40 minutes with no impact. The checklist caught two similar
migrations from other teams that quarter."

REFLECTION
"I now test data migrations against production-scale copies and assume anything that
touches a big table needs batching."
