## Requirements

**Functional**: send notifications via push (iOS/Android), SMS, email and an in-app inbox; templates with localisation; user preferences per category and channel; scheduled and bulk campaigns; delivery status.
**Non-functional**: OTPs delivered within seconds; transactional within a minute; campaigns of tens of millions without hurting the rest; no duplicates; respect preferences and regulations.

## Estimates

| Quantity | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Users | 100 M, ~50 M with active devices |
| Volume | transactional ~200 M/day ≈ 2,300/s; campaigns up to 50 M in an hour ≈ 14,000/s |
| Providers | APNs/FCM handle very high throughput; SMS providers often allow only hundreds/s per account — the real bottleneck and cost |
| Storage | in-app inbox: 50 M users × 100 messages × 500 B ≈ 2.5 TB — partition by user, TTL old items |

## High-level design

<Diagram id="notification-pipeline" caption="Producers submit through one API; the router decides channels and timing; priority queues feed channel workers that call providers with rate limits and failover; receipts flow back into tracking." />

<Steps>

1. **API**: producers send `{userId, category, template, data, idempotencyKey}` — not finished text, so wording and translations live in one place.
2. **Router**: load preferences (cached), drop duplicates, apply mutes, opt-outs, quiet hours (in the user's time zone) and frequency caps, choose channels and render templates.
3. **Queues by priority**: critical (OTP, security), transactional, bulk. Separate consumer pools guarantee capacity for critical messages.
4. **Channel workers**: call providers with timeouts, retries and per-provider rate limits; fail over to a secondary SMS/email provider on errors.
5. **Tracking**: store each send with status; ingest provider callbacks (delivered, bounced, opened); remove dead device tokens and suppress bounced addresses.

</Steps>

## Delivery details by channel

| Channel | Notes |
|---|---|
| Push (APNs/FCM) | Device tokens per installation; tokens expire — remove on "unregistered" responses; collapse keys to replace stale notifications; small payloads |
| SMS | Expensive and rate-limited; regional regulations (sender registration, templates for OTP in some countries); multiple providers for failover and cost routing |
| Email | Domain authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), unsubscribe headers, bounce and complaint handling, warm-up for new sending IPs |
| In-app | Inbox table per user plus real-time delivery over WebSocket/SSE when online ([Real-time communication](../../backend/realtime-communication/)) |

## Campaigns at scale

Large sends are **jobs**, not loops in a request: segment users, then enqueue in batches at a controlled rate onto the bulk queue ([Job scheduler](../job-scheduler/)). Spread sends over time (and respect time zones) to avoid self-inflicted traffic spikes when everyone taps the notification at once.

<Pitfalls>

- **One queue for everything** — a campaign delays OTPs by minutes.
- **No idempotency** — producer retries spam users.
- **Quiet hours in server time** instead of the user's time zone.
- **Ignoring provider feedback** — sending to dead tokens and bounced emails hurts deliverability.
- **Hard-coded message text** in producers — no localisation or consistency.
- **Unbounded fan-out** from a request handler.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Centralise routing: preferences, dedup, quiet hours, caps and templates in one place.
- Separate priority queues; critical messages bypass user mutes and never wait behind bulk.
- Rate-limit and fail over per provider; track delivery and clean up invalid destinations.
- Treat campaigns as throttled background jobs.

</KeyTakeaways>
