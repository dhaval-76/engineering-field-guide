## Why it matters

Video is most of the internet's traffic. A streaming platform must accept large uploads, transcode each video into many resolutions and bitrates, cut them into small segments, and deliver them from CDNs so playback starts fast and adapts to the viewer's connection without buffering. The design is dominated by processing pipelines, storage and CDN delivery, with adaptive bitrate streaming (HLS/DASH) at its core.

<Callout type="analogy">
A film released as a set of pamphlets in several sizes. Each scene is printed in small, medium and large print, and the cinema hands you scenes in whichever size your eyesight — your bandwidth — can manage at that moment, switching between sizes scene by scene.
</Callout>

## Requirements

**Functional**: creators upload videos; viewers watch on any device and network; seek, resume, captions; view counts; (live streaming as a deep dive).
**Non-functional**: playback starts in under ~2 s and rarely stalls; uploads of many GB survive flaky connections; global audience; processing within minutes; cost-efficient at enormous egress.

## Estimates

| Quantity | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Uploads | assume 500 hours of video per minute |
| Storage per hour of video | all renditions of the ladder below sum to ≈ 16.7 Mbps ⇒ ≈ 7.5 GB per hour |
| Daily new storage | 500 h/min × 1,440 min × 7.5 GB ≈ **5.4 PB/day** (plus the original) |
| Playback | 1 B views/day × ~10 min × ~3 Mbps ≈ 225 PB/day of egress — why CDNs and caching dominate the design |

## High-level design

<Diagram id="video-pipeline" caption="Uploads land in object storage; a pipeline splits, encodes in parallel into every rendition, packages segments and manifests, and publishes them for CDN delivery to adaptive players." />

<Steps>

1. **Upload**: resumable multipart upload with presigned URLs ([File storage](../file-storage/)); create a video record in "processing" state.
2. **Split**: cut the source at keyframes into pieces (e.g. 1 minute) so encoding parallelises.
3. **Encode**: a job per (piece × rendition) on a worker fleet; a DAG workflow tracks completion and retries failures ([Workflow engine](../workflow-engine/)).
4. **Package**: produce CMAF segments and HLS/DASH manifests; apply DRM encryption for premium content; generate thumbnails and captions.
5. **Publish**: write outputs to object storage, mark the video ready, warm the CDN for expected hits.

</Steps>

## Delivery and cost

- **CDN** serves segments with long TTLs (immutable files) and manifests with short TTLs ([CDN](../../distributed-systems/cdn/)); signed URLs or tokens protect paid content.
- **Popularity tiers**: the head of the catalogue is cached at the edge (Netflix even places caches inside ISPs); the long tail lives in cheaper storage tiers and is fetched on demand.
- **Encode once, serve many**: spending more compute on better compression pays off for popular titles because egress dominates cost.

## Live streaming

Ingest over RTMP/SRT to regional servers; transcode in real time into the ladder; publish segments continuously with a sliding-window playlist. Latency is roughly segment duration × buffered segments — standard HLS ≈ 15–30 s; **low-latency HLS/DASH** with partial segments reaches ~2–5 s; sub-second needs WebRTC. Chat and reactions use [real-time communication](../../backend/realtime-communication/).

## View counts and analytics

Players emit heartbeat events; a stream pipeline de-duplicates, filters bots, and aggregates counts in windows ([Real-time analytics](../realtime-analytics/)). Exact counts can lag; approximate live counters are fine. The same events feed quality-of-experience metrics (startup time, rebuffer ratio, average bitrate) and [recommendations](../recommendation-system/).

<Pitfalls>

- **Streaming whole files** instead of segments — no adaptation, no seeking efficiency, poor caching.
- **Encoding sequentially** — a 2-hour film takes hours; split and parallelise.
- **Starting playback at high quality** — slow start and early stalls.
- **Jumping several rungs up at once** — oscillation and rebuffering.
- **Counting views synchronously** in the playback path.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Upload resumably, process asynchronously, encode in parallel into a bitrate ladder.
- Segments + manifests turn video into cacheable files for CDNs.
- The player adapts per segment using throughput and buffer.
- Egress dominates cost — cache the head, tier the tail, compress popular titles harder.

</KeyTakeaways>
