## Why it matters

Payments are where "mostly correct" is not acceptable: a request retried after a timeout must not charge twice, money must never appear or vanish between accounts, and every rupee must be explainable months later in an audit. A payment system wraps unreliable external providers (card networks, UPI, banks) with idempotent APIs, a double-entry ledger, explicit state machines and daily reconciliation. Interviewers use it to test correctness under retries and failures more than raw scale.

<Callout type="analogy">
A bank branch's double-entry ledger. Every movement is written twice — out of one account, into another — so the books always balance, mistakes are fixed by new entries rather than erasing old ones, and at the end of the day the branch reconciles its ledger against the central bank's statement line by line.
</Callout>

## Requirements

**Functional**: customers pay merchants by card/UPI/wallet through external payment service providers (PSPs); wallet-to-wallet transfers; refunds; merchant payouts; transaction history and statements.
**Non-functional**: correctness over everything (no double charges, no lost money, full audit trail); 1 M+ transactions/day with peaks during sales (~1,000/s); high availability for accepting payments; strong consistency for balances; regulatory compliance (PCI DSS, data residency).

1 M transactions/day is only ~12/s on average — volume is rarely the hard part; correctness under retries, timeouts and partial failure is.

## High-level design

## Idempotency everywhere

- Clients send an `Idempotency-Key` with every create/charge/refund/transfer; the server stores key → request hash → outcome and returns the stored outcome on retry ([Idempotency](../../backend/idempotency/)).
- Calls to PSPs carry **our own** idempotency key, so retrying a timed-out charge can't charge twice.
- Webhook and event consumers de-duplicate on the provider's event id.

## The ledger

<CodeTabs id="ledger" title="A double-entry ledger and an atomic, idempotent transfer" />

- Every movement is a **journal transaction** with two or more **entries** (account, direction, amount) whose debits equal credits — money only moves between accounts, including external ones like "PSP clearing" or "fees".
- Entries are **append-only**: corrections are new reversing entries, never updates or deletes — this is the audit trail.
- Balances = sum of entries; for speed, keep a balance column updated in the **same transaction** and verify it against entries periodically.
- Store amounts as integers in minor units (paise, cents) with a currency; never floating point.

## Payment lifecycle with an external provider

<Walkthrough id="payment-lifecycle" />

<CodeTabs id="psp-webhook" title="Charging through a PSP with timeouts and idempotent webhooks" />

- A timeout from the PSP is **unknown**, not failure: mark the payment `PENDING_CONFIRMATION`, then resolve it by the webhook or by querying the PSP with the same idempotency key. Never retry a charge with a new key.
- Webhooks are at-least-once and may arrive out of order: verify the signature, de-duplicate by event id, and apply only **valid state transitions**.
- Refunds and chargebacks are their own state machines and ledger transactions.

## Across services

- Within one service: one database transaction updates the payment, writes ledger entries and inserts an **outbox** event — so state, money and notification never disagree ([Microservices](../../distributed-systems/microservices/)).
- Across services (order, payment, inventory): an **orchestrated saga** — the payment step is explicit, its state stored, and compensations defined (refund if fulfilment fails). Two-phase commit doesn't work with external providers and couples availability.
- Concurrency on hot accounts (a merchant receiving thousands of payments): lock rows in a consistent order, keep transactions short, or shard a hot account into sub-accounts and aggregate ([Payment thread-safety](../../concurrency/case-payment-thread-safety/)).

## Reconciliation

Daily (or more frequent) jobs compare three views — our ledger, our payment records, and the PSP's settlement report — matching by our payment id and PSP reference. Mismatches (missing webhook, amount difference, unexpected fee, chargeback) open exceptions for automated fixes or manual review. Reconciliation is what catches the bugs that every other mechanism missed.

## Security and compliance

- **Never store card numbers**: card data goes straight from the client to the PSP's hosted fields/SDK; you store a token. This keeps most of your system out of PCI scope.
- Encrypt sensitive data, restrict and audit access, sign and verify webhooks, rate-limit and run risk/fraud scoring before authorising.
- Keep data where regulations require it (for example, payment data localisation rules).

## When things fail

Interviewers almost always ask "what happens when X goes down?". Decide the degraded behaviour in advance: For services that publish events, the general patterns — circuit breakers, spooling, ordered replay — are in [Surviving dependency outages](../../distributed-systems/failure-fallbacks/).

| What fails | What users see | What the design does |
|---|---|---|
| The provider times out on a charge | "processing" status | never assume failure: mark it pending, query the provider by idempotency key, and let webhooks or reconciliation settle it |
| A webhook from the provider is lost | payment stays pending | poll pending payments after a delay; daily reconciliation against the provider's report catches the rest |
| The client retries after a timeout | no double charge | the idempotency key returns the original result |
| The ledger database is down | payments rejected | fail closed — never accept money you can't record; the payment page shows a clear retry message |
| Kafka (payment events) is down | nothing | events are in the outbox table, committed with the payment; the relay publishes them later |

<Pitfalls>

- **Treating a timeout as a failure** and retrying with a new key — double charges.
- **Floating-point money.**
- **Updating balances without ledger entries**, or editing entries — no audit trail.
- **Trusting the synchronous API response alone** and ignoring webhooks.
- **Publishing events outside the database transaction.**
- **Applying webhook events without checking state transitions** — a late "failed" overwrites "captured".
- **No reconciliation.**

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Idempotency keys on every write, inbound and outbound.
- Double-entry, append-only ledger in integer minor units; balances derived or updated atomically.
- State machines advanced by verified, de-duplicated webhooks; timeouts mean "unknown".
- Local transactions + outbox; orchestrated sagas across services.
- Reconcile against provider reports; tokenise card data.

</KeyTakeaways>
