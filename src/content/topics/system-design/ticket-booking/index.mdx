## Why it matters

When tickets for a big concert or match go on sale, demand can be thousands of times the supply, all within minutes. The system must never sell a seat twice, must hold seats while people pay without letting abandoned carts lock them forever, and must survive a traffic spike that would crush a normal site. The design covers atomic seat holds, a virtual waiting room, <Term id="sharding">sharding</Term> inventory by event and payment with expiring holds.

<Callout type="analogy">
A theatre box office on opening day. A queue outside lets a few people in at a time; each customer's chosen seats get a "reserved" ticket clipped to the seating chart while they pay, and if they wander off, the clip is removed after ten minutes so someone else can buy those seats.
</Callout>

## Requirements

**Functional**: browse events and shows; view a seat map; select seats; hold them while paying; confirm and issue tickets; cancel and refund.
**Non-functional**: never sell a seat twice; stay responsive during on-sales with millions of simultaneous users; fair ordering; resist bots.

The object model — shows, seats, holds, bookings — is in [Design a movie booking system](../../low-level-design/movie-booking/). This page is about **scale and contention**.

## Estimates

| Quantity | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Normal traffic | 10 M bookings/day across thousands of events ≈ 120/s |
| Hot on-sale | 2 M fans for 60,000 seats within ~10 minutes |
| Hot-event reads | seat map refreshes from 2 M clients — must come from cache/CDN, not the seats DB |
| Hot-event writes | holds ≤ seats × a few attempts — tens of thousands, but concentrated on the best rows |

## Holding seats without double booking

The hold must be one atomic check-and-set per seat — a conditional `UPDATE` (only if available or the previous hold expired), a <Term id="redis">Redis</Term> `SET seat:{show}:{seat} {user} NX EX 480`, or both. Multi-seat holds are all-or-nothing in one transaction.

<CodeTabs id="movie-booking-sql" title="Conditional UPDATE as the hold, with lazy expiry (from the LLD design)" />

- **Expiry** returns seats from abandoned checkouts; payment callbacks arriving after expiry are refunded rather than confirmed.
- **<Term id="idempotency">Idempotent</Term> payment** with a key per booking attempt ([Payment system](../payment-system/)).
- **General admission** (no seat numbers) is a counter per section — use the bucketed counters from [inventory management](../../low-level-design/inventory-management/).

## Surviving on-sales: the waiting room

<Walkthrough id="onsale" />

<CodeTabs id="waiting-room" title="A waiting room: idempotent joins, batch admission per tick, positions and expiring admission" />

- The queue page is static and CDN-served, so millions of waiting clients cost almost nothing; clients poll their position occasionally.
- Admission rate is set from booking-system capacity and remaining inventory; admitted users get a **signed, expiring token** checked by the booking API ([CDN signed URLs](../../distributed-systems/cdn/) use the same idea).
- Randomise order among users who arrived before the sale opened, so refreshing at 09:59:59.9 isn't an advantage; enforce one queue entry per verified account.
- Close the queue as soon as sold + held reaches capacity, and tell waiting users.

## Read path and sharding

- **Seat maps**: cache availability per section with a <Term id="ttl">TTL</Term> of a second or two and update from hold events; tolerate brief staleness — the hold decides.
- **Shard by event (show)**: holds for one event live in one partition, keeping transactions local; a hot event can get dedicated capacity.
- **Search and browse** come from a read-optimised catalogue (search index + cache), separate from inventory.

## Bots and fairness

Rate limits per account, device and IP ([Distributed rate limiter](../distributed-rate-limiter/)); CAPTCHA or proof-of-work at queue entry; verified accounts; purchase limits per account; and anomaly detection on holding patterns (many holds, no payments).

## When things fail

Interviewers almost always ask "what happens when X goes down?". Decide the degraded behaviour in advance:

| What fails | What users see | What the design does |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic far beyond capacity | a place in a waiting room | a virtual queue admits users at a rate the inventory service can handle |
| A user abandons checkout | seats free up after minutes | holds expire automatically (TTL) and return seats to sale |
| Payment fails or times out | seats held until expiry | the hold stays until it expires; the payment is reconciled before seats are released if the outcome is unknown |
| The inventory store fails | sale pauses | fail closed: selling without the source of truth risks double booking; resume from the waiting room |

<Pitfalls>

- **Check availability, then book** in two steps — double booking under load.
- **Holds without expiry** — seats locked forever by abandoned carts.
- **Serving seat maps from the primary database** during an on-sale.
- **Letting everyone into the booking flow at once** — the system collapses and nobody gets tickets.
- **First-come by request timestamp** at the opening second — rewards bots and refresh spamming.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Atomic, expiring holds prevent double booking; payment confirms or the hold lapses.
- Waiting rooms convert an impossible spike into a steady, fair flow.
- Shard inventory by event; cache seat maps; the hold is the source of truth.
- Plan bot defence and fairness from the start.

</KeyTakeaways>
