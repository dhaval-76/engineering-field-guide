## Why it matters

A feed shows each user the recent, relevant posts of the people they follow, in milliseconds, at the scale of hundreds of millions of users. Building it at read time means gathering posts from hundreds of accounts per request; building it at write time means copying each post into millions of follower feeds when a celebrity posts. The classic answer is a hybrid, and the interesting part of the interview is the trade-off between write amplification, read latency and freshness.

<Callout type="analogy">
A newspaper with personalised editions. Writers' stories can be slipped into each subscriber's copy as they're filed (fan-out on write), or each subscriber's copy can be assembled when they pick it up (fan-out on read). For a writer with ten million subscribers, slipping a copy into every paper is impossible, so those stories are added at pickup.
</Callout>

## Requirements

**Functional**: post text/media; follow users; home feed of followees' posts (ranked or reverse-chronological), infinite scroll; like/comment counts.
**Non-functional**: 300 M DAU, ~20 feed loads per user per day, ~100 M new posts/day; feed load <Term id="p99">p99</Term> < 300 ms; a new post visible to followers within seconds; skewed graph (a few accounts with tens of millions of followers).

## Estimates

Feed reads ≈ 6 B/day ≈ 70,000/s (peaks 3–5×). Posts ≈ 1,200/s. If the average account has 200 followers, push fan-out = 1,200 × 200 ≈ 240,000 feed inserts/s — fine — but one post by an account with 50 M followers is 50 M inserts on its own. Feed cache: 300 M users × 800 ids × 8 bytes ≈ 2 TB of RAM across a <Term id="redis">Redis</Term> cluster (only active users need cached feeds).

## Fan-out strategies

| Aspect | Push (on write) | Pull (on read) | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post cost | O(followers) inserts | O(1) | O(followers) for normal accounts only |
| Read cost | read one list | query every followee, merge | one list + a few celebrity timelines |
| Freshness | seconds (async) | immediate | seconds |
| Weak spot | celebrities; inactive followers waste work | users following thousands | merge logic and thresholds |

Refinements: skip fan-out to users inactive for 30+ days (rebuild on their return); treat "celebrity" as a follower-count threshold with hysteresis; for very active readers, cache the merged first page briefly.

## Storage

<CodeTabs id="feed-model" title="Posts, follows and the feed cache" />

- **Posts** in a store partitioned by author (Cassandra/DynamoDB or <Term id="sharding">sharded</Term> SQL), media in object storage behind a <Term id="cdn">CDN</Term>.
- **Follow graph**: two tables (followers of X, followees of X) for both directions of lookup.
- **Feed cache**: a Redis sorted set per user of post ids scored by time (or rank), trimmed to a bounded length. The cache is derived data — it can always be rebuilt from follows + posts.

## Reading a feed

<CodeTabs id="feed-read" title="Read: cached ids + celebrity posts, cursor pagination, batch hydration" />

- **Cursor** = (score, post id) of the last item returned; next page = items strictly older. Offsets duplicate or skip items as new posts arrive ([Pagination](../../backend/pagination/)).
- **Hydrate in batches**: multi-get post bodies, authors and counts from caches; filter deleted posts, blocked/unfollowed authors and privacy changes at read time.
- **Ranking**: retrieve a few hundred candidates (followees' posts plus recommendations), score with features (recency, affinity, engagement, content type) — a model service — then diversify and paginate the ranked list per session.

## Counts and interactions

Likes and views are high-write, low-precision: increment sharded counters in Redis (or a counter service), flush aggregates to durable storage periodically, and show approximate numbers. Whether *you* liked a post is exact and stored per user.

## When things fail

Interviewers almost always ask "what happens when X goes down?". Decide the degraded behaviour in advance:

| What fails | What users see | What the design does |
|---|---|---|
| The feed cache is lost | slower first loads | feeds are rebuilt on read from the follow graph and recent posts; warm the most active users first |
| Fan-out workers fall behind | new posts appear late | the queue absorbs the spike; pull-on-read merges recent posts for active readers |
| Ranking service is down | a less personalised feed | fall back to reverse-chronological order |
| A celebrity posts | nothing unusual | celebrity posts are pulled at read time, so no million-way fan-out happens |

<Pitfalls>

- **Synchronous fan-out in the post request** — a celebrity's post would take minutes.
- **Storing post bodies in every feed** — edits and deletes become impossible; store ids.
- **Offset pagination** on a changing feed.
- **Fan-out to inactive users.**
- **Joining follow graph and posts on every read** for users with thousands of followees.
- **Exact global counters on the hot path.**

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Hybrid fan-out: push for most accounts, pull for celebrities, merge on read.
- Feed caches of ids are derived and rebuildable; hydrate in batches.
- <Term id="cursor-pagination">Cursor pagination</Term>; filter deletions and blocks at read time.
- Counts are approximate and sharded; ranking happens after candidate retrieval.

</KeyTakeaways>
