## Requirements

**Functional**: customers register HTTPS endpoints and choose event types; every matching event is POSTed to each endpoint; customers can see delivery attempts, rotate secrets, and replay events.
**Non-functional**: never lose an event; deliver within seconds normally; tolerate customer endpoints being slow or down for days; one bad endpoint must not delay others; secure (authentic payloads, no SSRF into our network).

## Estimates

| | |
|---|---|
| Events | 50 M/day ≈ 600/s average, ~3,000/s peak |
| Fan-out | ~2 endpoints per event ⇒ ~6,000 deliveries/s at peak |
| Concurrency | at 300 ms average response, ~1,800 requests in flight; slow endpoints (10 s timeouts) need per-endpoint caps so they can't occupy all workers |
| Retry backlog | if 1% of endpoints are down for a day: ~1 M pending jobs — store them durably, not in memory |

## High-level design

<Diagram id="webhook-pipeline" caption="Events arrive through an outbox-fed stream, fan out into per-endpoint delivery jobs, and are sent by workers that sign, time out, retry with backoff, and eventually give up into a replayable failed list." />

<Steps>

1. **Capture events reliably** with the transactional outbox ([Sagas and outbox](../../architecture/saga-and-outbox/)) — an order committed always produces its event.
2. **Fan out**: look up subscriptions by event type and tenant; create one delivery job per endpoint, stored durably (a jobs table or a queue with delayed delivery).
3. **Deliver**: workers pick due jobs, POST with a short timeout, record the status code, latency and response snippet.
4. **Retry**: on non-2xx or timeout, compute the next attempt with backoff + jitter; after the retry window, mark failed.
5. **Observe and control**: delivery logs, per-endpoint success rate, a dashboard with replay, automatic disabling and customer alerts.

</Steps>

## Signing and verification

<CodeTabs id="webhook-signing" title="HMAC signatures with timestamps, constant-time verification, and the retry delay function" />

- Sign `timestamp.rawBody` with a per-endpoint secret; send `Webhook-Signature: t=…,v1=…` and `Webhook-Id`.
- Receivers verify over the **raw bytes** (re-serialised JSON differs), compare in constant time, and reject timestamps older than a few minutes to stop replays.
- Support **secret rotation**: sign with both old and new secrets (`v1=…,v1=…`) during a grace period.

## Isolation and fairness

- **Per-endpoint queues or concurrency limits** — a customer whose endpoint takes 10 s gets, say, 5 concurrent deliveries, not all workers.
- **Circuit breaker per endpoint** — after repeated failures, pause deliveries and probe occasionally instead of hammering a dead server ([Resilience patterns](../../architecture/resilience-patterns/)).
- **Fair scheduling across tenants** — round robin over tenants with due jobs so a burst from one doesn't starve others.

## Ordering

Ordering isn't guaranteed: retries and parallel workers reorder events. Include a creation timestamp and a per-object version or sequence so receivers can ignore older updates, or keep deliveries per (endpoint, object) sequential when ordering truly matters — at the cost of head-of-line blocking. Thin events ("order 42 changed — fetch it") sidestep ordering by making receivers read current state.

## Security

- **SSRF**: customer-supplied URLs could target internal services or cloud metadata. Resolve DNS, reject private, loopback and link-local ranges, connect to the resolved IP (no re-resolution), disallow redirects to internal addresses, and send from dedicated egress IPs customers can allow-list.
- HTTPS only; no secrets in URLs; payload size limits; redact sensitive fields per event type.

<Pitfalls>

- **Sending webhooks inside the business transaction** — slow customers slow your API, and failures roll back orders.
- **One shared queue for all endpoints** — one slow customer delays everyone.
- **Retrying without jitter** — synchronized retry storms after an outage.
- **No event id** — receivers can't de-duplicate at-least-once delivery.
- **Following customer URLs to internal addresses** (SSRF).
- **Silently dropping** after retries — customers need a failed list and replay.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Outbox → fan-out → one durable delivery job per endpoint, with backoff, jitter and a long retry window.
- Sign timestamp + raw body; receivers verify and de-duplicate by event id.
- Isolate endpoints with per-endpoint limits and circuit breakers; disable dead ones.
- Guard egress against SSRF; give customers logs and replay.

</KeyTakeaways>
