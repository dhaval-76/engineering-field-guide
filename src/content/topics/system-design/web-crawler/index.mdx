## Requirements

**Functional**: starting from seeds, fetch pages, extract links, discover and crawl new pages; store page content for indexing; recrawl pages to keep them fresh.
**Non-functional**: politeness (never overload a site, obey robots.txt); scale to billions of pages; robustness against malformed pages and traps; extensibility (new content types, processors).

## Estimates

| Quantity | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Target | 1 B pages per month ≈ **400 pages/s** average, plan for ~1,000/s |
| Bandwidth | ~500 KB average page ⇒ 200–500 MB/s inbound |
| Storage | 1 B × 500 KB ≈ 500 TB/month raw; compressed ~5× ⇒ ~100 TB/month |
| URL "seen" set | 10 B URLs × ~100 B as a hash set ≈ 1 TB; as a 1% Bloom filter ≈ 10 B × 9.6 bits ≈ **12 GB** |

## The frontier: priority and politeness

The frontier must balance two things: crawl important pages first, and never hit one host too often. The classic Mercator design uses **front queues** by priority feeding **back queues** per host, with a heap of "next allowed time" per host.

<CodeTabs id="frontier" title="URL normalisation and a polite frontier: per-host queues and next-allowed times" />

- **Politeness**: one connection per host at a time, a delay between requests (robots `Crawl-delay`, or adaptive — back off when response times rise or 429/503 appear).
- **Priority**: PageRank-like importance, depth, sitemap hints, freshness needs.
- **Scale**: partition the frontier by **hash of host** across workers ([Consistent hashing](../../distributed-systems/consistent-hashing/)), so each host's politeness is enforced in one place without coordination.

<CodeTabs id="bloom" title="A Bloom filter with double hashing, sized from the expected count and target false-positive rate" />

- **URL dedup**: normalise first (case, default ports, fragments, tracking parameters, parameter order), then check the Bloom filter; keep an exact store of crawled URLs for recrawl scheduling.
- **Content dedup**: exact duplicates by content hash; near-duplicates (mirrors, boilerplate changes) by **SimHash** — similar documents have fingerprints that differ in few bits.

## Robustness

- **Traps**: infinite calendars, session ids in URLs, `?page=1..∞`, symlink loops. Limit depth, URL length and pages per site; detect repeating path patterns.
- **Bad content**: size limits, timeouts, content-type filtering, defensive HTML parsing in sandboxed workers.
- **DNS**: a local resolver cache — DNS lookups otherwise dominate latency.
- **Failures**: fetch results and frontier state are persisted; workers are stateless and restartable ([Job scheduler](../job-scheduler/)).

## Freshness

Pages change at very different rates: news front pages every minute, archives never. Track change history per URL (content hash per fetch) and schedule recrawls proportional to estimated change rate and importance, using HTTP conditional requests (`If-Modified-Since`, `ETag`) to make unchanged pages cheap. Sitemaps' `lastmod` hints help.

<Pitfalls>

- **Ignoring politeness** — sites block you, or you take them down.
- **Not normalising URLs** — the same page crawled under dozens of URLs.
- **Exact hash sets for billions of URLs** in memory — use Bloom filters plus disk-backed stores.
- **No trap limits** — crawlers stuck forever in generated URL spaces.
- **Uniform recrawl intervals** — stale news and wasted fetches of static pages.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- The frontier combines priority with per-host politeness; partition it by host.
- Normalise URLs and de-duplicate with Bloom filters; de-duplicate content with hashes and SimHash.
- Guard against traps and bad content; cache robots.txt and DNS.
- Recrawl by estimated change rate and importance.

</KeyTakeaways>
