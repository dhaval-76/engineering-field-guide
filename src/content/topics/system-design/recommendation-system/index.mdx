## Requirements

**Functional**: personalised home recommendations; "similar items" on product pages; recommendations update as users interact.
**Non-functional**: < 200 ms per request; 100 M users, 10 M items; fresh signals within minutes; safe to experiment (A/B tests).

## Estimates

| | |
|---|---|
| Requests | 100 M users × 10 page views/day ≈ 12,000 requests/s average, ~40,000 peak |
| Events | ~100 interaction events per user per day ⇒ 10 B/day ≈ 115,000 events/s into the stream |
| Item embeddings | 10 M × 128 floats × 4 B ≈ 5 GB — fits in memory per ANN replica |
| Ranking cost | ~1,000 candidates × model inference per request — batch on CPU/GPU, budget ~50 ms |

**Embeddings** generalise it: a two-tower model learns a vector for each user and item so that likely interactions are close; serving retrieves the nearest item vectors to the user vector from an approximate-nearest-neighbour index (HNSW), exactly the machinery used for [RAG retrieval](../../ai-engineering/rag/). Other sources — trending, recently viewed, follows, editorial — are merged and de-duplicated.

## Ranking and re-ranking

- **Ranking model** (gradient-boosted trees or a neural network) predicts click, purchase or watch time from user, item and context features; multi-objective scores are combined with tunable weights.
- **Feature store**: the same feature definitions computed for training (from history) and serving (fresh counters from the stream), so the model sees consistent inputs.
- **Re-ranking**: business rules and slate-level goals — remove purchased or unavailable items, diversity across categories and sellers, freshness boosts, exploration slots, sponsored placements.

## Data, training and evaluation

- **Log impressions** with positions and outcomes; unclicked impressions are the negatives. Without them models can't learn what users ignore.
- **Training** runs daily (or continuously) on logs; correct for position bias (items at the top get clicked more regardless of relevance).
- **Offline metrics** (recall@k for candidates, NDCG/AUC for ranking) filter ideas; **online A/B tests** on click-through, conversion, watch time and retention decide.
- **Feedback loops**: recommending only what's popular makes it more popular; keep exploration and diversity in the slate.

## Cold start

- **New users**: popular and trending items by region and context, onboarding preferences, quickly adapting to the first clicks in-session.
- **New items**: content-based features (category, text and image embeddings) place them near similar items; reserve exploration traffic to collect signal.

<Pitfalls>

- **Scoring the whole catalogue per request** — use a funnel.
- **Training only on clicks** without impressions — no negatives, biased models.
- **Different feature code** for training and serving — silent skew.
- **Optimising click-through alone** — clickbait wins, satisfaction drops; balance objectives.
- **No exploration** — new items and niche tastes never get a chance.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Recommendations are a funnel: many cheap candidates, a learned ranker, rule-based re-ranking.
- Collaborative filtering and embedding retrieval generate candidates; vector indexes serve them.
- A feature store and impression logs connect training and serving.
- Decide with A/B tests; plan for cold start, diversity and feedback loops.

</KeyTakeaways>
