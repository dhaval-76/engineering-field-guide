## Why it matters

Production fails in ways tests never imagined, and you can't attach a debugger to it. What you can do is ask questions of the telemetry your code emits — if you emitted the right telemetry. This page is the developer's side: what to emit from your code and how to use it. The platform that collects and stores it at scale is covered in [Logging and monitoring](../../system-design/logging-monitoring/).

<Callout type="analogy">
A car's dashboard, a GPS trip log and a mechanic's notes. The warning light (metric) says something is wrong. The trip log (trace) shows the route and where you stopped for 40 minutes. The mechanic's notes (logs) explain it was a flat tyre on the motorway. You need all three, and they must share the same trip id.
</Callout>

## Three signals

| Aspect | Metrics | Traces | Logs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit | Numeric time series | Tree of timed spans for one request | Timestamped events |
| Cost | Low, fixed per series | Medium — usually sampled | High — volume grows with traffic |
| Answers | Is it broken? how badly? trend? | Which hop is slow or failing? | What exactly happened and why? |
| Typical tools | Prometheus, Micrometer, OTel metrics | OTel → Jaeger, Tempo, vendors | JSON logs → Loki, Elasticsearch |

**Correlation** is what makes them work together: the trace id is propagated across HTTP (`traceparent` header) and messages, recorded on spans, and written into every log line; metrics can link to example traces (exemplars).

## Instrumenting a service

<CodeTabs id="instrument" title="One operation instrumented with a span, a counter, a latency histogram and a structured log line with the trace id" />

<Steps>

1. **Auto-instrumentation first** — OpenTelemetry agents and framework integrations cover inbound HTTP, outbound HTTP, JDBC, Kafka and <Term id="grpc">gRPC</Term> with no code.
2. **Add business spans and metrics** for the operations you care about: checkout, payment authorisation, orders placed by channel.
3. **Structured logs** (JSON) with stable field names; log events, not prose; include ids (order, user — hashed if sensitive), never secrets or full card numbers.
4. **Low-cardinality metric labels** (route template, status class, channel). User ids, order ids and raw URLs belong on spans and logs — as labels they create millions of series.
5. **Sample traces** (head sampling at 1–10%, or tail sampling that keeps all errors and slow requests).

</Steps>

## What to measure

- **RED** for every service and endpoint: request **R**ate, **E**rrors, **D**uration (as a histogram).
- **USE** for every resource (thread pools, connection pools, queues, CPU, disk): **U**tilisation, **S**aturation (queue length, wait time), **E**rrors. Pool saturation is often the first sign of trouble ([Connection pooling](../../backend/connection-pooling/)).
- **Business metrics**: orders per minute, payment success rate — they catch failures technical metrics miss.

## SLIs, SLOs and alerting

- **SLI**: a measurement of user experience — fraction of requests that succeed, fraction faster than 500 ms.
- **SLO**: the target — 99.9% of checkout requests succeed over 30 days. **Error budget**: the allowed 0.1% — spend it on releases and experiments; when it's exhausted, prioritise reliability.
- **SLA**: a contractual promise with penalties, looser than the SLO.
- **Alert on burn rate**: page when the budget is being consumed fast enough to matter (14× the sustainable rate over an hour, or 6× over six hours), open a ticket for slow, steady burns. CPU at 90% isn't an outage; users failing is.

## Debugging with the signals

<Walkthrough id="incident-debugging" />

<Pitfalls>

- **Unstructured, uncorrelated logs** — grep across 40 pods with no trace id.
- **User ids or URLs as metric labels** — cardinality explosion, huge bills, slow queries.
- **Averages** instead of percentiles from histograms.
- **Alerting on causes** (CPU, memory) instead of symptoms (errors, latency) — alert fatigue.
- **Logging secrets, tokens or personal data.**
- **Pages without runbooks**, and incidents without blameless postmortems.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Metrics detect, traces locate, logs explain — joined by the trace id.
- Use OpenTelemetry; auto-instrument, then add business spans and metrics.
- RED for services, USE for resources, percentiles from histograms, low-cardinality labels.
- Define SLOs and alert on error-budget burn rate.

</KeyTakeaways>
