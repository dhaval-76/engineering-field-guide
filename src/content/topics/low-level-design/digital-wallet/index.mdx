## Why it matters

A wallet looks like `balance -= x; balance += x`. The interview is about everything that can go wrong around those two lines: retries that pay twice, concurrent transfers that overdraw or <Term id="deadlock">deadlock</Term>, crashes between the debit and the credit, rounding errors, and auditors asking why a balance is what it is. The answers — double-entry ledgers, <Term id="idempotency">idempotency</Term> keys, lock ordering and holds — carry straight into real payment systems ([Payment system](../../system-design/payment-system/)).

<Callout type="analogy">
An accountant's ledger with two columns. Money never appears or disappears; every line that takes ₹50 from one account has a matching line that gives ₹50 to another. If the totals don't balance at the end of the day, something is wrong — and the ledger shows exactly where.
</Callout>

## Clarify

- Operations: top-up, P2P transfer, pay a merchant, refund, withdraw to bank? (All; top-up and withdrawal go through system accounts.)
- Holds (authorise now, charge later — rides, hotels)? (Yes.)
- Single currency? (Yes; multi-currency as an extension.)
- Limits and KYC? (Mention; out of scope for code.)
- Consistency? (Strong — no overdrafts, ever.)

## Model

<Diagram id="wallet-model" caption="Accounts, transactions, balanced ledger entries and holds. System accounts (bank-in, bank-out, fees) let deposits and withdrawals balance too." />

- **Account**: cached `balance` and `held`; `available = balance − held`.
- **Transaction**: one business operation with a unique **<Term id="idempotency-key">idempotency key</Term>**.
- **Ledger entry**: (transaction, account, signed amount). Entries of a transaction sum to zero.
- **Hold**: an authorisation that reduces available funds until captured or released.

## Transfers

<Walkthrough id="wallet-transfer" />

<CodeTabs id="wallet" title="Wallet service: double-entry ledger, idempotent transfers, ordered locking, holds with atomic capture" />

Concurrency rules the code follows:

- **Ordered locking** — always lock the account with the smaller id first, so two opposite transfers can't wait on each other ([Deadlock](../../concurrency/deadlock-livelock-starvation/)).
- **Check and update under the lock** — the balance can't change between the check and the debit.
- **Claim the idempotency key atomically** and remove it if the transfer fails, so a corrected retry can proceed.
- **Capture is atomic with the hold release** — otherwise funds freed by the release could be spent before the capture runs. A hold is claimed (removed) before capture, so two captures can't both succeed.

## In the database

<CodeTabs id="wallet-schema" title="PostgreSQL: CHECK constraints, a UNIQUE idempotency key, ordered row locks, conditional debit and audit queries" />

- `CHECK (balance - held >= 0)` makes overdrafts impossible even if application code has a bug.
- The debit is a conditional update (`WHERE balance - held >= amount`); 0 rows updated means insufficient funds.
- Ledger rows are insert-only; corrections are new reversing transactions.
- For very hot accounts (a merchant receiving thousands of payments a second), split them into several sub-accounts or apply credits asynchronously from a queue — debits must stay strictly consistent; credits can lag slightly.

## Reconciliation

Run audits continuously: every transaction sums to zero, cached balances equal summed entries, and system accounts match bank and payment-gateway statements. Discrepancies raise alerts, never silent fixes.

<Pitfalls>

- **Floating-point money.**
- **Updating balances without a ledger** — no audit trail, no way to explain or rebuild.
- **Check-then-act without a lock or conditional update** — concurrent overdrafts.
- **Locking in request order** — deadlocks between opposite transfers.
- **No idempotency key** — client retries double-pay.
- **Releasing a hold and charging in two steps.**

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Double-entry: balanced entries per transaction; balances derived and audited.
- Idempotency keys, ordered locks or conditional updates, and DB constraints prevent double spends and overdrafts.
- Holds reduce available balance; capture and release atomically.
- Reconcile continuously against the ledger and external statements.

</KeyTakeaways>
