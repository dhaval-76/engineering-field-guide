## Why it matters

Food delivery is a favourite machine-coding problem at Indian product companies because it has three kinds of users with different powers over one shared object — the order — plus a matching problem (which partner?) and money (fees, surge, refunds). A clean answer separates the lifecycle, the matching and the pricing so each can change independently.

<Callout type="analogy">
A relay race with a baton. The restaurant holds the order first and must hand it over when it's ready; only the runner assigned to that lane can take it, and only they can cross the finish line. A referee (the state machine) disqualifies anyone who grabs the baton out of turn.
</Callout>

## Clarify

- Single restaurant per order? (Yes.)
- Who can cancel, and when? (Customer free until accepted; with a fee after; system on timeouts.)
- Partner assignment: on order placed or when the restaurant accepts? (On accept — partners don't wait long.)
- One order per partner at a time? (Yes; batching is an extension.)
- Pricing: delivery fee by distance, surge, coupons? (Yes.)

## Entities

| Entity | Key fields |
|---|---|
| Restaurant | id, location, open hours, menu, accepting orders |
| MenuItem | id, name, price (paise), available |
| Cart | customer, restaurant, items × quantity (one restaurant per cart) |
| Order | id, items snapshot, pickup/drop locations, amounts, **status**, partner, history |
| DeliveryPartner | id, location, available, rating, active orders |
| Payment | order, amount, status (authorised, captured, refunded) |

Snapshot prices into the order at checkout — later menu changes mustn't alter placed orders.

## Dispatch and pricing

<CodeTabs id="food-dispatch" title="Nearest-available dispatch strategy, a dispatcher listening for ACCEPTED, and a pricing function" />

- **Dispatch strategy** variants: nearest to restaurant; minimise total ETA (partner → restaurant → customer, accounting for prep time); batch two orders from the same restaurant; prefer partners finishing a nearby delivery. In production, candidates come from a geospatial index ([Proximity service](../../system-design/proximity-service/)).
- **Assignment races**: two orders must not grab the same partner — claim atomically (a set or a conditional DB update), and handle partner rejection by re-dispatching.
- **Pricing**: base fee by distance, surge multiplier by demand/supply in the area, discounts, small-order fees — a pipeline of rules, each a strategy. Money in paise.

## Robustness

- **<Term id="idempotency">Idempotent</Term> status updates**: partner apps on mobile networks retry; "DELIVERED twice" must be a no-op.
- **Timeouts**: restaurant doesn't accept in N minutes → reject and refund; no partner found → widen radius, raise incentive, or notify the customer.
- **Payments**: authorise at checkout, capture on delivery (or on accept), refund on rejection ([Digital wallet](../digital-wallet/) covers holds).

<Pitfalls>

- **Status as a free-form string anyone can set** — orders jump from PLACED to DELIVERED.
- **Assigning partners at order time** — they wait at the restaurant for 25 minutes.
- **Referencing live menu prices** from orders.
- **Pricing and dispatch logic inside the Order class.**
- **No handling for partner or restaurant no-shows.**

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Model the order as a state machine with explicit actor permissions.
- Dispatch and pricing are strategies; status changes are events.
- Snapshot prices, use integer money, and make status updates idempotent.
- Plan timeouts and re-dispatch for every waiting state.

</KeyTakeaways>
