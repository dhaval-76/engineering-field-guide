## Why it matters

A brokerage app has many parts — accounts, KYC, portfolios, charts — but interviews zoom in on the **order book and matching engine**, because that's where data-structure choice, correctness rules and concurrency all matter. The same price-time priority idea powers crypto exchanges, ad auctions and any marketplace that matches the best bid with the best offer.

<Callout type="analogy">
A queue at a market stall, but organised by price. Buyers willing to pay more stand at the front of the buyers' line; sellers asking less stand at the front of the sellers' line. Among people offering the same price, whoever arrived first goes first. A deal happens whenever the front buyer will pay at least what the front seller asks.
</Callout>

## Clarify

- Order types: limit and market first; stop-loss, IOC/FOK as extensions.
- One exchange process per symbol, or the whole platform? (Matching engine first; brokerage features are separate services.)
- Cancel and modify orders? Yes.
- Latency/throughput targets? Microseconds per order at exchanges; milliseconds at a broker's internal crossing engine.

## Entities

- **Order** — side, type, limit price (ticks), remaining quantity, arrival sequence, status.
- **Trade** — buy order, sell order, price, quantity.
- **OrderBook** — per symbol: bids, asks, id index.
- **Account / Portfolio** — cash, holdings, open orders; updated from trade events.

## The order book

<Walkthrough id="order-matching" />

<CodeTabs id="orderbook-engine" title="Price levels in sorted maps, FIFO within a level" />

The rules the code enforces:

<Steps>

1. **Crossing**: a buy crosses if its limit ≥ best ask; a sell crosses if its limit ≤ best bid. Market orders always cross while the opposite side has liquidity.
2. **Price-time priority**: the best price matches first; within a price level, the earliest order (lowest sequence) matches first.
3. **Trade price**: the **resting** order's price. An aggressive buy at ₹105 against a resting ask at ₹103 trades at ₹103 — price improvement for the incoming order.
4. **Leftovers**: a limit order's remaining quantity rests on its side of the book; a market order's remaining quantity is cancelled (it has no price to rest at).
5. **Cancel**: look up by id, remove from its price level, drop empty levels.

</Steps>

| Operation | Structure | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Best bid / ask | `TreeMap.firstKey()` | O(log P) (O(1) with a cached pointer) |
| Add at a price | `computeIfAbsent(price).addLast` | O(log P) |
| Fill against best | `peekFirst` / `pollFirst` | O(1) per fill |
| Cancel | `Map<id, Order>` + remove from level | O(1) lookup (+ level removal) |

Production engines often replace the tree with an **array indexed by price tick** around the current price (price levels are dense integers) and intrusive linked lists for O(1) cancel, avoiding allocation entirely.

## Concurrency: serialise per symbol

Matching must be strictly ordered — two threads matching the same book concurrently would break price-time priority. Rather than a lock per book, real systems give each symbol (or shard of symbols) **one thread** fed by a queue: deterministic, lock-free inside the book, and parallel across symbols.

<CodeTabs id="orderbook-threading" title="Single-threaded shards per symbol, plus a pre-trade risk check" />

Around the engine:

- **Pre-trade risk** (broker side): enough cash/margin for buys, enough holdings for sells, price bands, max order value.
- **Sequencing and recovery**: every accepted order and trade is written to a log in sequence; the book can be rebuilt by replaying it.
- **Market data**: trades and top-of-book changes are published as events to charts and tickers.
- **Circuit breakers**: halt a symbol if its price moves beyond a band within a time window; resume with a call auction.

## Extensions

- **Modify order** — reducing quantity keeps time priority; changing price or increasing quantity is cancel + new order (loses priority).
- **IOC / FOK** — immediate-or-cancel fills what it can now; fill-or-kill checks available quantity first and fills all or nothing.
- **Stop-loss** — held outside the book and injected as a market/limit order when the last trade price crosses the trigger.
- **Portfolio** — consumes trade events: update holdings and average cost, release blocked cash.

<Pitfalls>

- **`double` prices** — equality and rounding bugs in matching; use integer ticks.
- **Trading at the incoming order's price** instead of the resting order's.
- **Resting market orders** in the book.
- **Locking the whole book with `synchronized`** under high load — correct but a bottleneck; single-threaded per symbol is simpler and faster.
- **No id index** — cancel becomes a scan of the whole book.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Bids and asks are sorted price levels of FIFO queues: price first, then time.
- Match while crossing; trade at the resting price; rest limit leftovers, cancel market leftovers.
- Integer ticks, an id index for cancels, and one thread per symbol.
- Risk checks, sequencing logs and market-data events surround the engine.

</KeyTakeaways>
