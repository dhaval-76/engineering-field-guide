## Why it matters

Chess is the cleanest LLD demonstration of **polymorphism**: six piece types, one call (`candidateMoves`), and the game logic never asks "what kind of piece is this?". It also has a genuinely tricky rule — a move is only legal if it doesn't expose your own king — which tests whether you can separate a piece's movement rules from game-level rules. Other board games (tic-tac-toe, snakes and ladders, checkers) are simpler variations of the same structure.

<Callout type="analogy">
Each piece is a specialist who knows only how it moves. The referee (the game) asks each one "where could you go?", then applies the rules that involve the whole board — whose turn it is, and whether the move would leave the king in danger.
</Callout>

## Clarify

## Entities

<Diagram id="chess-classes" caption="Game owns the board, the turn and the move history. Pieces know only their own movement." wide />

- **`Square`** — an immutable (file, rank) value.
- **`Piece`** — colour plus `candidateMoves(board, from)`. Pieces don't store their own position; the board is the single source of truth, so a move can't leave the two out of sync.
- **`Board`** — 8×8 grid, lookups, "is this square attacked by colour X?".
- **`Move`** — from, to, moved piece, captured piece; can apply and revert itself.
- **`Game`** — turn, history, legal-move filtering, status.

## Move generation by polymorphism

<CodeTabs id="chess-model" title="Piece hierarchy with shared sliding logic" />

Notice the structure:

- **Rook, bishop and queen** differ only in their direction vectors, so they share `slide()`: walk each direction until the edge, an own piece (stop before) or an enemy (capture, then stop).
- **Knight and king** have fixed offsets; the knight jumps, so there's no path check.
- **Pawn** is the irregular one — direction depends on colour, two steps from the start rank, captures diagonally only.

In JavaScript, a table of move-generator functions does the same job without classes — pieces have no behaviour beyond move generation.

## Game rules: legality, check, mate

A piece's candidate moves aren't necessarily legal: moving a pinned bishop could expose the king. The simplest correct approach is **make the move, test, unmake**:

<CodeTabs id="chess-game" title="Legal moves, game status and undo" />

- **Check** — the side to move has its king attacked.
- **Checkmate** — in check and no legal move exists.
- **Stalemate** — not in check but no legal move exists (a draw).

`isAttacked` loops over all enemy pieces and asks for their candidate moves — simple and fast enough for an interview. A faster version scans outward from the king: along ranks/files for rooks/queens, diagonals for bishops/queens, knight offsets and pawn capture squares.

## Undo with the Command pattern

Because each `Move` records what it captured, `revert` restores the board exactly; `Game.undo()` pops the history stack and flips the turn. The same history gives you move lists (PGN), replays, and "takeback" in online play. Special moves need to store a little more state to revert correctly:

| Special move | Extra state the move must remember |
|---|---|
| Castling | Rook's from/to squares; castling rights before the move |
| En passant | The captured pawn's square (not the destination) |
| Promotion | The original pawn (to restore) and the promoted piece |
| Any move | Previous castling rights, en-passant target, half-move clock |

## Extensions

- **Online play** — a `GameSession` per match, moves validated on the server, events pushed to both players; store moves, not boards.
- **Timers** — a `Clock` per player that starts/stops on each move.
- **AI player** — a `Player` interface with `HumanPlayer` and `EnginePlayer` (minimax with alpha-beta over `legalMoves`).
- **Other games** — tic-tac-toe is a 3×3 board, one piece type, and a win check over rows, columns and diagonals; the `Game`/`Board`/`Move` skeleton carries over.

<Pitfalls>

- **Switching on piece type** (`if (piece instanceof Knight)`) in the game logic — defeats the design.
- **Pieces storing their own position** as well as the board storing pieces — two sources of truth drift apart.
- **Forgetting the king-safety rule** — candidate moves aren't legal moves.
- **Undo that only moves the piece back** — loses captured pieces and special-move state.
- **Checking only "in check" for game end** — stalemate is also an end state.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Pieces implement their own movement; the game applies board-wide rules.
- Legal = candidate move that leaves your king safe (simulate, test, revert).
- Checkmate and stalemate both mean "no legal moves"; check decides which.
- Moves as Commands give undo, history and replays.

</KeyTakeaways>
