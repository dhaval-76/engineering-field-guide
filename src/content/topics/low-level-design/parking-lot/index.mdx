## Why it matters

Parking Lot is the single most frequently reported LLD question. It looks simple, which is the point: it tests whether you clarify scope, model entities cleanly, put interfaces where things vary, and — the part many candidates skip — make allocation safe when many cars arrive at once. The same shape (typed resources, allocation, a ticket with a lifecycle, pricing) reappears in seat booking, meeting rooms, lockers and car rentals.

<Callout type="analogy">
A multi-storey car park with an attendant at each gate. Each attendant looks at the board, picks a free space for your vehicle size, hands you a ticket and marks the space taken. The hard part isn't the board — it's making sure two attendants never send two cars to the same space.
</Callout>

## Step 1 — Clarify

Questions worth asking, with the answers this design assumes:

| Question | Assumed answer |
|---|---|
| Floors? Entry/exit gates? | Several floors, several gates operating concurrently |
| Vehicle types? | Motorcycle, car, truck |
| Spot sizes and fitting rules? | SMALL, MEDIUM, LARGE; a vehicle can use its size or larger |
| Pricing? | Hourly by vehicle type, with a daily cap; pluggable |
| Payment? | Pay before exit; payment gateway out of scope |
| Reservations, EV charging, valet? | Out of scope initially — good extensions to discuss |
| Availability display? | Yes, per floor and size, updated live |

## Step 2 — Entities and relationships

<Diagram id="parking-classes" caption="Core classes. The lot owns spots and tracks active tickets; spot selection, pricing and display updates are behind interfaces." wide />

- **`ParkingSpot`** owns its occupancy and the atomic claim. Spots know their floor and size.
- **`Ticket`** is the record of one stay: vehicle, spot, entry time, status and fee. Its status moves `ACTIVE → PAID → CLOSED` and rejects anything else.
- **`ParkingLot`** coordinates: selects candidates, claims, issues tickets, and notifies listeners. It holds no pricing or selection logic itself.

<Callout type="tip" title="Size rules as data, not subclasses">
A `Car`/`Bike`/`Truck` class hierarchy adds little here — vehicles have no different *behaviour*. An enum plus a small fitting table (`SpotRules.fitsIn`) is clearer and easier to extend. Use subclasses when types genuinely behave differently.
</Callout>

Things worth pointing out as you code:

- **Selection is a strategy.** "Nearest to the entrance", "smallest fitting spot first", "spread load across floors" and "reserved-first for members" are all just different `SpotSelector`s.
- **Pricing is a strategy.** Weekend rates, event rates or flat fees are new `PricingPolicy` classes. The fee is computed once at payment and stored on the ticket, so a rate change later doesn't alter past receipts.
- **Time is injected** through a `Clock`, so tests can fast-forward three hours without sleeping.
- **Money** is a value type (integers in the smallest unit, or `BigDecimal`), never `double`.

## Step 6 — Concurrency: the last free spot

Several gates call `park()` at the same moment. If "find a free spot" and "mark it occupied" are separate steps, two threads can find the same spot and both mark it. The fix is to make the **claim** atomic; finding candidates doesn't have to be.

<Walkthrough id="parking-last-spot" />

Options, from coarse to fine:

| Approach | How | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| One global lock | `synchronized park()` | Correct and simple; all gates queue behind each other |
| Lock per floor | `ReentrantLock` per floor around find+claim | Gates on different floors proceed in parallel |
| CAS per spot | `occupant.compareAndSet(null, v)` (shown above) | No blocking; a loser just tries the next candidate |
| Concurrent free-spot set | `ConcurrentSkipListSet.pollFirst()` per size | Atomic removal **and** O(log n) nearest-spot lookup |

The last option also fixes performance: instead of scanning thousands of spots per arrival, keep one sorted set of free spots per size.

<CodeTabs id="parking-free-index" title="O(log n) allocation with a sorted concurrent set per size" />

<Callout type="warning" title="When the lot runs on more than one server">
Atomic variables only protect memory inside one process. With gates served by several application instances, the claim must happen in shared storage: `UPDATE spots SET vehicle=? WHERE id=? AND vehicle IS NULL` (check the affected-row count), a Redis `SET spot:F1-M7 KA-01 NX`, or by partitioning floors between instances so each owns its spots.
</Callout>

## Extensions interviewers ask for

- **Reservations** — add `RESERVED` to spot state and a `Reservation(spot, window, holder)`; walk-ins skip spots with an active reservation; a scheduled job releases no-shows after a grace period.
- **EV charging spots** — a spot *feature* (`chargerKw`) the selector can require, plus a charging fee component in pricing. No changes to `ParkingLot`.
- **Lost ticket** — look up the active ticket by plate (keep a `plate → ticketId` index) and charge a lost-ticket fee policy.
- **Multiple exits with payment kiosks** — `pay()` and `exit()` are already separate; exit gates only verify `PAID`.
- **Monthly passes** — a `PricingPolicy` that returns zero for valid pass holders, chosen per vehicle.

<Pitfalls>

- **Find-then-mark without atomicity** — the classic double-booking bug.
- **`double` for money** and `toHours()` truncation (`61 minutes → 1 hour`): use integer minutes, round up explicitly, and a money type.
- **Singleton `ParkingLot` with static access** — makes tests share state. Construct it once and inject it.
- **A god `ParkingLot`** containing pricing tables, display rendering and payment calls. Keep those behind interfaces.
- **Recomputing fees at exit** after a rate change — store the fee on the ticket when paid.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Model spots, vehicles and tickets; make ticket status a small state machine.
- Spot selection and pricing are strategies; display boards are observers.
- Make the spot claim atomic (CAS or a concurrent free-spot set); across servers, move the claim into the database or Redis.
- The same design generalises to any "allocate a typed resource and bill for it" problem.

</KeyTakeaways>
