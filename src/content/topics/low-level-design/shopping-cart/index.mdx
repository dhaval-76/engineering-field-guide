## Why it matters

A cart looks like a list with add and remove. The interesting parts are **pricing** — promotions that interact, rounding, tax order — and **checkout**, where stale prices, stock running out and double-clicks all have to be handled. Interviewers use it to see if you can keep business rules pluggable and money exact, and whether you can make a multi-step checkout safe.

<Callout type="analogy">
A shopping basket holds items, not price stickers. At the till, the cashier scans each item at today's price, applies offers in a fixed order, adds tax, and only then takes payment — after checking the items are actually in stock.
</Callout>

## Clarify

- Logged-in and guest carts? Both; merge at login.
- Promotion types: percentage above a threshold, flat coupons, buy-X-get-Y, category discounts? Yes, and new ones will keep coming.
- Can promotions stack? Defined per group: one order-level percentage, one coupon, item promos independent.
- Tax: on the discounted amount; per-category rates possible.
- Stock reservation at checkout? Yes, with a timeout.

## The cart

<CodeTabs id="cart-model" title="Cart lines as SKU + quantity, with guest merge" />

Store **intent**, not prices. Prices change, promotions expire and the client can't be trusted to send prices — so the server prices the cart from the catalogue whenever it's displayed, and authoritatively at checkout. Persist logged-in carts in a database (they survive devices); guest carts can live in Redis with a TTL keyed by a session id, merged into the user's cart at login.

## Pricing pipeline

<Walkthrough id="cart-pricing" />

<CodeTabs id="cart-pricing" title="Promotions as strategies in an ordered pipeline" />

Design choices that matter:

- **Promotions are strategies** — `PercentOffAbove`, `FlatCoupon`, `BuyXGetYFree`, and next quarter's ideas are new classes. The engine doesn't change.
- **Order of application** is explicit: item-level offers first, then order-level percentages, then fixed coupons, then tax. Changing the order changes the total, so it's a business decision, not an accident of code.
- **Stacking policy**: promotions belong to groups; within a group only the best applies. Across groups they stack. A `maxDiscountPercent` cap guards against unintended combinations.
- **Every adjustment is recorded** with a label — the breakdown is what the receipt, refunds and customer support rely on.
- **Exact money**: integers in paise; percentage discounts round in the customer's favour (or per policy), consistently.

<Callout type="tip" title="Buy X get Y: which units are free?">
Expand the eligible units, sort by price, and make the **cheapest** ones free. Otherwise a customer buying one expensive and two cheap items gets more discount than intended.
</Callout>

## Checkout

<CodeTabs id="cart-checkout" title="Idempotent checkout with all-or-nothing stock reservation" />

<Steps>

1. **Idempotency** — the client sends a `checkoutId`; a repeat returns the existing order instead of charging again.
2. **Re-price** the cart on the server with currently active promotions; if the total changed since the user saw it, show the new total before charging.
3. **Reserve stock** for every line atomically (`UPDATE … SET available = available - qty WHERE available >= qty`, SKUs in sorted order). If any line fails, roll back and tell the user which items are short.
4. **Charge** with the checkout id as the payment idempotency key.
5. **Create the order** with the price breakdown; **commit** the reservation; clear the cart.
6. On any failure, **release** the reservation. Reservations also expire on their own.

</Steps>

"Check stock, then reserve" in two separate steps is racy — the conditional update *is* the check.

## Extensions

- **Price changed while in cart** — show a notice on the cart page and require confirmation at checkout.
- **Saved for later / wishlist** — another list keyed by user; no pricing.
- **Coupon usage limits** — per-user and global redemption counts updated atomically when the order is created.
- **Multiple sellers** — split the cart into per-seller shipments at checkout; promotions may be seller-scoped.

<Pitfalls>

- **Trusting client-sent prices** or prices stored when the item was added.
- **`double` money** and inconsistent rounding across lines.
- **Implicit promotion order** buried in code — totals change unexpectedly when someone reorders a list.
- **Check-then-reserve stock** in two steps — overselling under load.
- **Non-idempotent checkout** — double charges on double-click or retry.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Cart = SKUs and quantities; prices come from the catalogue at pricing time.
- Promotions are strategies in an explicit, ordered pipeline with a stacking policy; record every adjustment.
- Checkout: idempotency key, re-price, reserve all-or-nothing with conditional updates, charge, commit or release.

</KeyTakeaways>
