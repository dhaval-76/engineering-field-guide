## Choosing an algorithm

| Graph | Algorithm | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Unweighted (every edge 1) | BFS | O(V + E) |
| Weights 0 or 1 | 0-1 BFS (deque: weight-0 edges to the front) | O(V + E) |
| Non-negative weights | **Dijkstra** with a min-heap | O((V + E) log V) |
| Negative weights, or "at most k edges" | **Bellman-Ford** | O(V · E), O(k · E) |
| DAG (any weights) | relax in topological order | O(V + E) |
| All pairs, small V | Floyd-Warshall | O(V³) |
| One target, good heuristic (maps) | A* | depends on heuristic |

<Callout type="analogy">
Dijkstra is water flooding from the source through pipes of different lengths: it reaches nearby junctions first, and the moment water arrives at a junction, no other route can get there sooner. A negative-length pipe would break that guarantee — water could arrive later by a "shorter" route.
</Callout>

## Dijkstra

<CodeTabs id="dsa-dijkstra" title="Network delay time — Dijkstra with a min-heap and lazy deletion" />

- **Relaxation**: if `dist[u] + w < dist[v]`, update `dist[v]` and push `(dist[v], v)`.
- **Lazy deletion**: the heap may hold outdated entries for a node; skip any popped entry whose distance is larger than the recorded one.
- Track `prev[v]` when relaxing to reconstruct the path.

## Bellman-Ford and "at most k stops"

Each round relaxes every edge using distances from the **previous** round, so after round i, distances use at most i edges. That makes it natural for limited hops — and it tolerates negative weights.

<Pitfalls>

- **Dijkstra with negative edges** — silently wrong answers.
- **Not skipping stale heap entries** — extra work, or wrong results if you relax from them.
- **Plain Dijkstra for "at most k stops"** — a cheaper path with too many stops can block a valid one; use Bellman-Ford rounds or put the stop count in the state.
- **Updating distances in place within a Bellman-Ford round** when the edge limit matters — copy the previous round.
- **Integer overflow** adding to `Integer.MAX_VALUE` as "infinity".

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Match the algorithm to the weights: BFS, 0-1 BFS, Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford.
- Dijkstra: min-heap, relax, skip stale entries; non-negative weights only.
- Bellman-Ford rounds bound the number of edges and detect negative cycles.

</KeyTakeaways>
