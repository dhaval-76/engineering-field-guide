## Recognise it

- "**How many ways**…", "**minimum/maximum**…", "**can we**…" over a sequence, string, grid or budget.
- A backtracking solution would reach the **same state by different paths** (same index and remaining amount).
- Choices at each step: take/skip, which coin, match/insert/delete.

<Callout type="analogy">
Counting the ways to climb a 10-step staircase taking 1 or 2 steps at a time. The ways to reach step 10 are the ways to reach step 9 plus the ways to reach step 8. Rather than re-counting step 8 from scratch every time it's needed, write each answer on the wall as you go and read it back.
</Callout>

## The recipe

<Steps>

1. **State**: what does `dp[i]` (or `dp[i][j]`) mean, in words? This is the hardest and most important step.
2. **Transition**: how is a state computed from smaller ones? (Often: try each choice, combine with min/max/sum.)
3. **Base cases**: the smallest states whose answers are known directly.
4. **Answer**: which state holds the final answer?
5. **Order**: evaluate states after the ones they depend on (bottom-up), or recurse with a memo (top-down).

</Steps>

## Longest increasing subsequence

`dp[i]` = length of the longest increasing subsequence ending at i → O(n²). The patience-sorting trick keeps, for each length, the smallest possible tail and binary-searches where each number goes → O(n log n).

## Two strings: LCS and edit distance

`dp[i][j]` describes prefixes `a[0..i)` and `b[0..j)`. If the last characters match, extend the diagonal; otherwise take the best of dropping a character from one side (and, for edit distance, replacing).

## Families at a glance

| Family | State | Transition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-D sequence | best up to/ending at i | from i−1, i−2… | climbing stairs, house robber, decode ways |
| Unbounded knapsack | best for amount a | try each item, `dp[a − w]` | coin change |
| 0/1 knapsack | best for items [0..i) and capacity c | skip, or take once `dp[i−1][c − w]` | subset sum, partition equal subset |
| Subsequence | best ending at i | all j < i with a valid link | LIS, longest chain |
| Two sequences | prefixes i, j | match / skip one / replace | LCS, edit distance, interleaving |
| Grid | best reaching (r, c) | from top and left | unique paths, minimum path sum |
| Interval | best for range [i, j] | split point k | burst balloons, matrix chain |

<Pitfalls>

- **Vague state definitions** — write the meaning in a comment before coding.
- **Wrong iteration order** — counting combinations vs permutations in coin change depends on whether coins or amounts are the outer loop.
- **0/1 knapsack in 1-D** iterating capacity upward — items get reused; iterate downward.
- **Recursion limits** in top-down Python/Java for large n — convert to bottom-up.
- **Using DP when greedy or a formula suffices** — and vice versa.

</Pitfalls>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Define the state in words; derive transition, base cases and order from it.
- Memoisation and tabulation compute the same states; choose by convenience and limits.
- Recognise the families: sequence, knapsack, subsequence, two-string, grid, interval.
- Optimise space by keeping only what the next step reads.

</KeyTakeaways>
